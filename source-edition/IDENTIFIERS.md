# Registry conventions

## Records and files

**A15-D02** identifies a record in dossier A15. **S** denotes an email record; **D** denotes a document, data or media record. Dossier titles and filenames use A01–A32. Identifiers remain stable. The former A33 is numbered A32 in this edition.

The [catalogue](CATALOGUE.tsv) has one row per record; the [file register](SOURCE_FILES.tsv) has one row per source file. A record can have several files, and one file can serve several records. Email text may appear directly in its dossier, without a separate file. Page, row, passage and message numbers locate evidence within the supplied source sequence.

## Extent of reproduction

The catalogue’s `representation` field describes what can be read or viewed here. `full`, `full-text`, `full-treated` and `redacted-text` preserve the supplied document or sequence, with any redactions shown; they do not promise an unredacted original. Image, video and audio entries identify the medium. `extract` and the qualified extract labels indicate partial reproduction. Fragment and metadata labels identify the limited surviving content. `withheld` means no source content is reproduced.

A facsimile preserves the page or image; a text extraction or OCR does not preserve its layout. A video frame is not a recording. The individual entry records these distinctions. [████] marks redaction; […] marks an omitted passage.

## Conversations

Message and row numbers are edition locators, not verified platform identifiers. In the redacted private conversations, account names, dates, identity keys and reply links are omitted. “Message” does not identify a continuing speaker; “Operator” identifies the compiler’s retained statements and “Bot” generated output. Quoting a public source does not establish who wrote the message.

The public Discord witness retains its supplied labels and earlier pseudonymisation. Labels and repeated messages do not establish a number of people, members or voters. Public authors and performers may remain named.
