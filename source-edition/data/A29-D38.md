Use a simple tactile push button from a hardware store, or even repurpose a spring and foil contacts inside a cardboard enclosure:

Open an old mouse and locate the left-click switch on its circuit board.
De-solder or remove any other switches you don’t need.
Solder two wires to the left-click’s input/output leads.
Extend those wires to a momentary push button or foil contact inside a minimal custom enclosure (cardboard, plastic, or wood).
Reassemble or embed the mouse board in your sculptural shell, keeping the new button accessible.
Example:

Hot-glue the bare PCB onto a sturdy piece of cardboard.
Attach a small spring-loaded push button where the left-click switch would normally be.
Cover it with a simple cardboard façade, leaving just the new “toggle” protruding.
Plug the mouse’s USB cable into your computer; a single press registers as a left-click.

DESCRIPTION

This is a playtest for broad listening. 

Playtest means it’s not a finished artwork. It does mean it’s been developed far enough to merit testing with exhibition guests. We wish to do this in order to develop the AI system and the overall interaction of the upcoming artwork. 

We are playing with the concept of broad listening by civic hacker Audrey Tang. Broad listening has been described as a change from broadcasting into broad listening, the latter being an aspiration to listen to a broader base of people, also those you disagree with. 

This concept has been combined in this playtest [████]. The aim of the playtest is to develop a large-scale immersive installation in the future.
