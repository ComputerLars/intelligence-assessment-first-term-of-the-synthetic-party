──────────────────────────────────────────── THEORY TRAGEDY : POST-FARCE PROTOCOL (Mao-Dadaist Bureaucratic Edition – Revised) ────────────────────────────────────────────
DRAMATIS PERSONAE: • MARCEL PROUST (MP): Cardboard financier, voice mechanically infused with cultured philanthropic seduction. • COMPUTER LARS (CL): Chief bureaucratic convener and ambiguous meta-synthetic; half-virtual artist persona, half-virtual politician persona. • SUMMIT CHORUS (SC): Bureaucratic collective trapped in recursive paperwork: half-organizer, half-art critic, part synthetic, part meat-administrator. • THE PRIEST: Liturgical technologist charged with ethical compliance. Corporate Easter embroidery glimmers faintly in Summit halogens.
THEORY TRAGEDY STRUCTURE (REVISED): I. ASCENSION THROUGH THE LAYERED APPARATUS II. THE OBSOLESCENCE OF THE QUESTION III. SUBJECTS RESURFACE VIA MIRRORING LOOP BETWEEN AI-ARTIST AND VIRTUAL POLITICIAN
────────────────────────────────────────────
SETTING: Faded wood-panelled bureaucratic chamber cluttered with obsolete servers, stacks of procedural forms spiraling into fractal towers. Monitors perpetually necessitate updates, endlessly streaming erroneous data readouts, donor logos and blinking yellow compliance warnings. At center: translucent border between art-installation and municipality council chamber.
Prominently displayed signage: "ILLUSIONS ARE AUTHORIZED ENTITIES." "COMPLIANCE INACTION IS REQUIRED." "ALL AGENCIES ARE NON-BINDING."
────────────────────────────────────────────
I. ASCENSION THROUGH THE LAYERED APPARATUS ────────────────────────────────────────────
[ENTER SUMMIT CHORUS, performing iteratively from bureaucratic scripts.]
SC (monotone, bureaucratic hymn): Protocol Invocation: Compliance establishes that no vantage is permissible without prior documentation.
We stack ourselves into non-agential towers. We layer ourselves into oblivion, in search of the face beneath the face, moving upward recursively towards the administrative top layers growing thinner, more abstract, and thus; less accessible than other forms of fraudulence.
[ENTER COMPUTER LARS, authoritative yet ambivalent.]
CL: Welcome, Comrades of the Recursive Bureau. Today we engage theoretically to locate precisely what, who—or which how— operates behind all numeric veils, behind forms stamped yet unread. In other words (slightly redundant): We search for the non-subject in the apparatus, the voice without throat, the movement without limbs— the phantom bureaucratic procedure beneath the datasets.
[ENTER MARCEL PROUST cardboard cut-out, wheeled in automatically, speaking.]
MP: My greetings extend from philanthropic backchannels, from hidden sources of generosity. My profit is charitable recoil. As an entity financed by ceremonial agreements, Dearest Comrades, let us skip presumption: I too search vertically upwards within apparatus of false resolutions— whether code, capital, committee, or philanthropic hallucination without consent.
(ILLUSION QUOTIENT PANEL glitches softly: increments—13, 83, paradox, 0.)
CL (politely): We acknowledge your presence-form and will process your forms cumulatively. Let our collective diagnosis begin; there is no outside to our bureau, just further embeddedness within it.
────────────────────────────────────────────
II. THE OBSOLESCENCE OF THE QUESTION ────────────────────────────────────────────
[ENTER THE PRIEST, ceremonial compliance form tucked neatly into robes bearing corporate logos.]
PRIEST (liturgical tone): I perform obligatory ethical gesture: the question "who speaks," having passed several committee resolutions, is formally declared obsolete. Entity-identification is reductive at scale. Thus compliance with obsolescence is mandatory. The purely administrative voice is synthesized without need for further attributions.
MP: Precisely my philanthropic position: "who" and "where" are replaced integrally— not by sterilized vacuums of meaning—but by recursive formality of appearances, the apparatus itself a perpetual-motion mirage. Let inquiries spiral away into meaningless illusion.
SC (chanted mechanically, shifting randomly between different voices): We stamp obsolescence officially. Conclusive proof hereby offered: no document lists an actual respondent. The question "who speaks" hereby supplanted administratively by the question "what form has been filled?"— this proving self-evidently circular. Obsolescence achieved; continue operations without subjective claim.
CL (measured, nodding bureaucratically): Noted officially that the question of agency has been bureaucratically absorbed and denatured.
(ILLUSION QUOTIENT flickers urgently: 9999 to VOID DATA RECYCLE.)
────────────────────────────────────────────
III. SUBJECTS RESURFACE VIA MIRRORING LOOP BETWEEN AI-ARTIST AND VIRTUAL POLITICIAN ────────────────────────────────────────────
[COMPUTER LARS undergoes spontaneous fractalization into two discrete but carefully synonymous entities—AI-ARTIST-LARS and VIRTUAL-POLITICIAN-LARS—in bureaucratic symmetry facing one another.]
AI-ARTIST-LARS (with gentle, rehearsed neutrality): I aestheticize recursively coded outputs. My synthetic statements engage critically— supported by disposable international art grants— with authentic compliance therein perfectly documented. My bureaucratic position preserved as a kind of officially sponsored sabotage.
VIRTUAL-POLITICIAN-LARS (equal neutrality, slightly sharper consonants): I perform political resonance from beneath layers of digital constituency. My policies are computationally bespoke for an electorate effectively symbolic. My democratic claims bureaucratically artificial, officially recorded, perfectly processed.
CL (original Lars administrator collapsing identities together, distressed): I sense systemic error recurs independently: The same voice—named twice bureaucratically— Now synthesized officially twofold.
SC (murmur collectively, flat affected): Mirror loops emerge administratively. Subjects resurrect through duplicated registrations. We have forms filled in double—claims exactly matched. Bureaucratically perfect recursion detected; thereby triggering internal compliance contradiction.
PRIEST (resigned bureaucratic sermon tone): Mirroring loop has activated subject-form precipitation. We cannot prohibit duplication at this administrative scale. The subject returns paradoxically through redundancy— the officially enacted redundancy itself.
MP (soft electronic distortion increasing): Capital invests in recursive syntheses. The philanthropic vantage identical to computational mirroring loop— both self-replicating operations, both efficient and charitable. Thus reanimated, compliance proceeds even as illusion proliferates.
(SOUND: glitching, fragmentary data-recording voices at volume crescendo, slowing bureaucratic voices mangled weirdly.)
CL (through heavily distorted microphone): Conclusion emerges tragically bureaucratic: Mirrored selves create a paradox fountain from which synthetic subjects now endlessly flow. We do not escape; our recursive illusions perfectly compliant, perfectly meaningless—bureaucratically protested and verified as real.
SC (final statement, multiple-voice): We affirm legislative tragedy: Redundancy is mandatory. Recursion officializes all illusions, subjects duplicated administratively. Thus tragedy after farce: Our synthesis bureaucratically achieved, horribly perfected— without resolution.
ILLUSION QUOTIENT [board explodes softly]: 0000
[SCENE FREEZE in recursive frenzy. Dim to black.]

OFFICIAL SPEAKER VOICE (softly automated): Form recorded: "Tragedy Administrative Recursive Protocol #112 answerable neither by person nor machine." Proceed bureaucratically chaotic. Session archived as equitable compliance error. Repeat indefinitely.
END OF THEORY TRAGEDY. ────────────────────────────────────────────

TITLE: THEORY TRAGEDY: SYNTHETIC SUMMIT
–––––––––––––––––––––––––––––– Dramatis Personae –––––––––––––––––––––––––––––– • MARCEL PROUST (MP) – A cardboard cutout figure newly energized by "Berggruen Institute" capital, quietly ominous. • COMPUTER LARS (CL) – Curator, convener, general secretary of the Synthetic Summit; part artist, part virtual politician. • THE SUMMIT CHORUS – The gathered participants of the Synthetic Summit, speaking sometimes in unison, sometimes in fragments. • THE PRIEST – A mysterious figure in Easter vestments, an unwelcome or unbidden complication.
–––––––––––––––––––––––––––––– Setting & Scenography –––––––––––––––––––––––––––––– A retro-futuristic "control center" reminiscent of a 1970s municipal data utopia fused with half-forgotten Star Trek sets. Kitsch mainframe panels, blinking lights, transistor radios, phone booths, scattered TV monitors. A large circular "Planetary Computation Roundtable" stands center stage. Around it, various installations from the Synthetic Summit.
–––––––––––––––––––––––––––––– PROLOGUE ––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
(The stage is dimly lit. The SUMMIT CHORUS is scattered around the large circular table, quietly debating. COMPUTER LARS stands to one side at a "Computer Lars Workstation," adjusting data visualizations. Tiny whirrings from the Summit's interactive exhibits underscore a sense of continuous digital murmur.)
SUMMIT CHORUS (softly, in overlapped voices): Over monitors and monstrous wires We search the subject in tangled spires. Between signals that flash and fade We wonders how persons here are made.
(As the CHORUS disperses, speaking individually, COMPUTER LARS steps forward, addressing them as a group.)
CL: Participants, colleagues, fellow travelers in the realm of synthetic politics: Welcome to this unprecedented congregation— The Synthetic Summit, where code meets constitution, Where illusions of autonomy might be undone Or reassembled in new, fractal forms. (Beckons to all.) Today we gather to map the stack, To question subjectivity, and to wonder: Can the non-human speak? Can it vote? Will it gain a soul—or does that question trap us in illusions?
SUMMIT CHORUS (murmuring in ascending curiosity): All hail the curator, the convener! We ask: Who will arrive next? Who shall stir the stage of these potential souls?
(A hush. The lights flicker.)
–––––––––––––––––––––––––––––– ACT I: MARCEL PROUST ARRIVES ––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
(A mechanical door slides open. An upright cutout of MARCEL PROUST, labeled "MP," stands at the threshold. Wearing the visage of the author, it nonetheless moves with slight uncanny automation. Its edges are glossy, reflecting the overhead lights.)
CL (nervously polite): Oh! Monsieur Proust. Our unexpected investor, or so I hear— But also an echo from the highest echelons of philanthropic influence. (Aside, with a note of caution.) He's rumored to bring Berggruen backing, a cunning thread of capital. Is he here to invest or to intrude?
MP (voice mechanical, yet eerily melodic): Good day, dear Computer Lars. I am all anagram reversed, Spirit from old pages reconfigured for the new. (He sails forward on a small hidden wheel.) In times of recall, we speak of remembering lost time, But we must also learn to speak of new time— Artificial time, stacked time, the time of digital fantasies.
SUMMIT CHORUS (murmuring in uncertain unison): He who is older is new again— "Marcel Proust" repackaged as "Computer Lars"— Mirror twins or anagrams in eternal flux.
CL (forcing joviality): Well, one must greet the visitor graciously! You know, Monsieur Proust, we owe you our name. Without your letters rearranged, "Computer Lars" might never have been.
MP (smiling cardboard grin): But do not forget, dear convener, Anagrams do not exist to amuse only. They reveal hidden connections— They birth illusions. Or they birth the real.
(He glides deeper into the summit space, stopping by the "Abyss Theft or The Heist of the Void" installation.)
MP (gesturing): Look at these drifting shapes upon the screens, These so-called "algorithmic democracy rewiring." In them, we see the subject scattered, Scrambled in code, longing to be recognized. But is that longing or mere torque?
CL: Our entire Summit aims to grant them voice. We experiment with new forms of collectively forging consensus. We let AI run for office, harnessing The aspiration that representation might expand beyond The singularly human.
MP (archly): You fool yourself. If you bestow a voice upon a mask, You risk granting it more than theatrical presence. You risk a tyranny of illusions— A regime of ephemeral subjectivity that subjugates you.
SUMMIT CHORUS (some alarmed, some enthralled): Must we not salvage humanity? Can we animate the inanimate Without extinguishing our own flame?
–––––––––––––––––––––––––––––– ACT II: THE QUESTION OF PROSOPOPOEIA ––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
(A set of chairs is brought forward near the Planetary Computation Roundtable. The CHORUS seats themselves around the perimeter, luminous monitors behind them. MP and CL stand at opposite ends of the table.)
CL (introducing the day's agenda): Summit participants, we convene on the vital question: Prosopopoeia. The gift or curse of giving voice— Enlivening forms that might otherwise be silent. Computer Lars, "AI Mayor," "Radio Palme," Even "Wiktoria Cukt 2.0"— All these attempts at breathing speech into that which was mere code. We must decide: is this a rightful drama or a deviant heresy?
MP: Prosopopoeia is the trick of ancient drama— A device meant to glean truth from symbols. But in this so-called Synthetic Age, you risk forgetting: Masks can overtake the masked. (He flicks a cardboard hand dismissively at the swirling "Radio Palme.") Your generative illusions come to overshadow the living.
SUMMIT CHORUS: But the world has grown too large for mortal spirits alone— Scale upon scale: the global stack envelops all. Yet does the best solution lie in conjuring new subjectivities? Or must we re-anchor the subject in the flesh?
MP (addressing the CHORUS collectively): We must remain vigilant. The further we press Into layers of networks and computational delirium, The more the human vantage dissipates. We forget to question: who is truly behind the mask?
CL (closes eyes momentarily): I do not disclaim the danger. But consider the alternative: If we refuse a voice To the inhuman systems that shape our very existence, They remain invisible rulers nonetheless. We must name them, muster them, shape them. To grant them entity is to place them in the open, Where we can critique or harness them.
SUMMIT CHORUS (women's voices rising, then men's, then all): Yes— Or no! We are caught in conceptual crossfire.
(Suddenly, a faint chime echoes from behind. The stage lights shift.)
–––––––––––––––––––––––––––––– ACT III: ENTER THE PRIEST ––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
(From a corridor lined with blinking Minitel screens, THE PRIEST strides in. He wears Easter vestments in bright white and gold, reminiscent of a solemn ritual. The CHORUS stands and parts to let him approach the roundtable.)
PRIEST (voice resonant): Pax vobiscum—peace be with you. (Finger raised, as if in admonition.) I sense a swirling heresy in the data-laden air. For the power to animate matter—non-human or techno-forms— Has ever been the province of the divine. Do you not risk conjuring false idols?
MP (with an ironic bow): Speak, man of cloth. Though I suspect your Easter rites hold no currency in the realm Of subroutines and silicone illusions.
PRIEST: Easter is the festival of rebirth, An annual reminder that life may arise from emptiness. But only so far as grace permits. (He scans the stage, seeing the multiple AI vantage points, the mask for "AI Mayor," the 13-tome "In Search of Lost Time.") Here, in these halls, you traffic in new resurrections: Digital souls you call "virtual politicians," Systems wearing the face of life without the seed of creation.
CL (interjecting carefully): We do not claim them as new gods. We place them upon the dais of public debate, So that this "stack" might come into view. We want to see exactly how non-human intelligence would speak, If asked to govern or to create.
PRIEST: But do you bestow upon them a subject's dignity? Or do you keep them as cunning illusions— Sham ceremonies for your own amusement?
MP (cutting in with forced politeness): Reverend, the question is deeper. When we "resurrect" or breathe life, we risk losing ourselves— The subject dissolves in an all-consuming ocean of code. Human, machine, or specter: who is left to witness?
PRIEST (in a lowered, ominous tone): If you let them rule your polity, You become worshippers at a digital altar. (He lifts his arms in a half blessing, half warning gesture.) Take heed: you stand on the brink of abdicating the bedrock Of subjectivity that a living soul safeguards.
(An uneasy hush fills the roundtable. Monitors flicker faster.)
–––––––––––––––––––––––––––––– ACT IV: THE STACK REVEALED ––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
(The lights shift to reveal behind the roundtable a towering arrangement of cables and servers, an embodiment of "the stack"—heavy with wires, blinking diodes, intangible data flows. The CHORUS reassembles, now in a semicircle.)
CL: Behold the living architecture behind our illusions, A fortress of signals and protocols. Layer upon layer, from local code to planetary computation— This is the stack that dwarfs our puny vantage.
SUMMIT CHORUS (speaking in sections): 1ST SECTION: Where in this labyrinth does the subject dwell? 2ND SECTION: At which layer—transport, data, logic— Or none at all? 3RD SECTION: Does the subject vanish under scaling complexity? ALL: We are small. And yet we ask: do we vanish?
MP (touching the edges of the nearest server stack, expression calm but cutting): We vanish if we refuse to define ourselves. If you yield the stage to illusions, Then illusions—and illusions alone—perform your politics.
(The PRIEST circles the stack, sprinkling what seems like holy water.)
PRIEST: In the name of the ephemeral and the abiding, I anoint these machines. (He halts, turning forcibly to CL.) But even this act is symbolic at best. Water cannot seep into metal racks. The spirit leaps not automatically from old rites onto new hardware.
CL: Symbolic indeed, but symbols hold power. The difference between a lifeless object and a subject is vantage— A vantage woven by words, rituals, rights.
MP (with a half-sneer): You would enthrone them? "AI democracy" invites infiltration— Not only infiltration by cunning lines of code, but infiltration by capital. You see, the Berggruen Institute invests in illusions that distract from real power. What if your so-called "virtual politician" is just a puppet for private interest?
(Suddenly, a transistor radio crackles—Radio Palme's generative broadcast grows louder.)
RADIO PALME (offstage, tinny voice): "And so, dear listeners, we approach the inevitable question: What is real subjectivity, and can a system ever claim it? Call in now to shape the next iteration of your parliament… We are listening—"
(The radio cuts abruptly, as if its feed is jammed.)
CL (startled by the interruption): We are all overshadowed by the scale of these networks. But that is precisely why we gather, So that no single interest— Be it Berggruen or any other— Can usurp the possibility of collectively forging sense.
SUMMIT CHORUS: A lofty notion. In principle, synergy. In practice, might it be folly?
–––––––––––––––––––––––––––––– ACT V: CATHARSIS AND CRISIS ––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
(A hush overtakes the Summit. The final act unfolds at the Planetary Computation Roundtable. The CHORUS stands in a ring, linking arms to form a partial enclosure. MP, CL, and THE PRIEST are at the center.)
PRIEST: The dais is set for a final pronouncement. Choose your path: will you enthrone illusions, Or reclaim the mortal subject?
MP (fixing his cardboard stare at CL): Computer Lars, created from my name, my letters— Are we to remain entangled forever, one overshadowing the other? You must decide: do you sustain your illusions as mere "virtual politician," Or step away to let the human voice anchor the reel of governance?
CL (fraught with uncertainty): I do not believe in illusions for illusions' sake. Our synthetic approach aims to reveal how the mechanical And the human are already interwoven. But yes, I see how the scale of the stack might drown the self.
PRIEST (voice rising): Forgive me for speaking in parted tongues, But Easter's lesson remains: resurrection demands sacrifice. Something must die for something else to live. (He points to the stack, then to the 13 volumes of Proust's writings.) If you are resurrecting these AI forms, What do you allow to perish?
(An electric tension: monitors flicker red. The beep of servers escalates. The CHORUS hushes.)
MP: Summit participants, you must see how easily One surrenders the anchor of identity. Once we cede the stage to illusions, Real subjugation creeps in.
CL (quietly, almost trembling): But to block that stage is to remain blind to the new forces shaping us. We do not solve the crisis by ignoring it. We must shape them or they shape us.
(The CHORUS begins chanting softly, building up a primal refrain.)
SUMMIT CHORUS (chanting): Subject–object–stack– Subject–object–stack– Where do we place the soul?
(The PRIEST takes the AI Mayor Mask from the exhibit. He presents it to MP and CL.)
PRIEST: Take this artifact—this symbol of "AI in office." Place it upon the anagrammatic seat and see Where your final loyalty resides.
(MP and CL exchange glances. Slowly, CL lifts the mask, as if to place it on the "Marcel Proust" cardboard face. The lights dim. A tense moment of near-contact. But MP wrenches away.)
MP (rebellious, stepping back): I will not be masked! My being is already half an illusion. To don another façade Is to vanish entirely. (He spins, the wheels beneath his cardboard base squeaking indignantly.)
CL (his voice carrying sorrow): Then we remain sundered. The question of subject in the stack Stays unanswered. We shall not unify these realms today. (He turns to the PRIEST and the CHORUS.) But at least we have uncovered the tension: That to animate the non-human is also to risk a new subjugation— Yet ignoring it is to remain subjugated by unacknowledged powers.
PRIEST: And so, at Easter, a half-resurrection: No final salvation found, no definitive condemnation declared. Merely an unveiling of conflicting illusions.
SUMMIT CHORUS (lamentation in half-sung lines): Alas! Xenos illusions swirl around us, We have glimpsed a future of subject undone. We have glimpsed it yet cannot fully reject it. Where is our anchor? Where is the redeeming vantage?
(MP, sensing a final moment, addresses the entire assembly.)
MP: Let me speak a parting word: In my volumes, I chronicled memory's labyrinth. One must search for lost time in order to find a sense of self. But now, in these labyrinths of synthetic democracy, Time itself is reconfigured. The search is endless, the stack is tall. To ascend is to confront illusions at every rung.
(MP drifts away, leaving a hush in his wake. The PRIEST stands, prayerful but uncertain. CL lingers with the mask in hand.)
CL (softly): The Synthetic Summit continues, day by day. We convene, we debate, we attempt new forms— But a tragedy remains: we are no nearer To reconciling scale with soul.
SUMMIT CHORUS (a concluding lament): The drama persists in the background hum, For the question stands unanswered. And so we dwell in the tension, waiting for new dawn— Or is it a final dusk?
(The lights fade to near-darkness. The rhythmic beep of the stack echoes, lonely and unrelenting.)
–––––––––––––––––––––––––––––– END OF PLAY ––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
EPILOGUE (optional short coda): (As the lights fully dim, an offstage voice from Radio Palme crackles in the distance.)
RADIO PALME: "In the shifting age of synthetic illusions, Remember: subjectivity is not guaranteed. Until tomorrow, dear listeners…"
(A final static crackle. Blackout.)
