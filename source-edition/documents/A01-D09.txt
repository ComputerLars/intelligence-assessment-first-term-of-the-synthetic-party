SUMMARY OF THE PROJECT PROPOSAL MADE BY GPT-3”The curator of a project that creates artificial minds, [the operator], explains how he will use “artificial stupidity” (AS) to make artificial minds (AM) come alive. [████] will use low-tech and theatrical techniques to make the artificial minds seem more real. The manual they are creating with text generation technology will be a fictional work that will explore the history of computer-assisted writing and the philosophical problems of artificial intelligence.”Our Artificial StupidityThe IDEAWe propose an exhibition project that constructs 'artificial minds' (AM's) by the use of language technologies. The idea is that creative work with new language models such as GPT-3 can showcase our present life with artificials.The project raises an alarming perspective of a world where "Life with Artificials" has not been included as the 18th SDG. We aim to raise awareness of how crude simulations of the mind influence everyday digital life. The constructed AM's are not to compete with human understanding but are reflections of how society utilises AI.Our method is to limit the AM's with 'artificial stupidity’ (AS). To make humans feel that an AM is authentic, one can use AS to 'dumb down' the AM and make it skilled at bias, slang, typos, etc. This is possible because language models are trained on data that is irrational, random and unpredictable (human writing). AS makes the reader-user feel that the AM has an identity and is not a robot. In an age where AI is used to target ads, scam, manipulate public narratives and influence opinion, there is a need to subvert misapplications of machine learning and artificial intelligence.The artworks that the project generates will make existing social biases (on and for AM's) present. It encourages playful, speculative fiction as well as poetic, provocative takes on reality. The project integrates well-known methods from art and literature (character building, style, narrative structure) with accessible technologies. We will solely use open-source language models (GPT-NEO, GPT-J, aitextgen, etc.) and cheap applications of GPT-3 software (AI-Dungeon, Sudowrite, Shortly-AI, etc.).Our take on AM's challenges scholarly dogma on what constitutes a mind. We dismiss conventional tests of whether AM’s understand math problems, semantics or ethics. Such tests can be disproven by changes in the ‘prompting’. To figure out the limit of an AM, one has to begin playing with it. Conventional thinking on artificial minds can only acknowledge AM’s as either companions or replacements of yourself. Instead, we evaluate whether AM’s can serve as a mirror. The best an AM can do today is turn on a feedback loop between what we think is human and what we accept as artificial.While the exhibition project explores the role of language for constructing believable ‘minds’, it will enact and embody the AM’s by all means necessary; digital, physical, low-tech and theatrical techniques will be employed to make the AM’s come alive.EXECUTIONInitially, we plan a project development phase which will take until early 2022 (1. October – 31. January). From there on, we will have produced the necessary means to seek external funding and to organise the exhibition along with a suitable venue. The planned exhibition will take place in late ’22 or early ’23.Project developmentIn the first four months, we will ensure a common ground for artist collaborations by producing a valuable product that is independent of the project outcome.The basis of the project will therefore be a “Manual for Making an Artificial Mind”. The Manual will be a literary work that is created together with text generation tech. The idea is to introduce language models as a flexible material for poetic, playful and critical writing and art. The Manual will be a resource for people to learn how to use AM’s in creative work, and thereby improve the state-of-the-art.[the operator] will direct the production of content. While the Manual will be a fictitious, computational experiment of its own, the reader-user will come to learn the history of real and imagined computer-assisted writing and the philosophical problems of the concepts artificial mind, artificial intelligence and artificial stupidity. The Manual develops instructions for the reader-user to foster new uses of language models.To tease the exhibition project, the Manual will throughout the development phase be uploaded to MindFuture’s webpage as a contribution to The Book of Mindsets.The Manual will serve as the exhibition’s vision statement. It offers participants a shared perspective and it will act as the template for external funding applications.
