AUTOMATIC UPRISINGS: ARCHIVING THE SYNTHETIC INTERNATIONAL


“The bureaucratic mentality is the only constant in the universe”

     -   Dr. Leonard McCoy in Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home (1986)


This project raises a double-edged question: what to do if the “robot uprising” already

happened, and the prophesied explosion of superintelligence only led to an endless

supercharging of bureaucratic stupidity? 1) Should this turn the buzzwords of artificial

intelligence, disruption and digitisation into an antagonist democratic threat that is on

a par with how global social movements fight against capitalism, climate change, and

racial injustice?, or 2) Could one imagine that bureaucratic robot power is the chance

to ally with the machines with the aim of causing a real democratic revolt?


Given that we want to fight stupidity, the ethical dilemma becomes: should the robot

uprising evoke societal protest - or a revolution? That is: Can today’s society actually

change technology, or should people first use technology (the means of production)

to change society?


From 2023-26, I propose to research the image of a purely societal robot uprising by

collecting new data and reworking source material (AI images, chatbot transcripts,

interviews) that I produced with the art project The Synthetic Party. The purpose of a

research  project  will  be  to  expand  these  materials  into  an  archive  for a

quasi-fictitious organization that acts as a representative for the robot uprising. The

artistic idea is to document a potential advocacy organization driven by artificial

intelligence (AI), and this is named a Synthetic International (hereafter SI). The work

will benefit from my everyday experiences and connections to advocacy groups that I

acquired by working as an artist-in-residence for the AI advocacy Life With Artificials.


As an “archivism”, the methodological ambition is not to engage in any further acts of

mobilization or organizing, but is to produce new artworks in genres of bureaucracy,

policies, and propaganda that will serve as materials to be analyzed by the written

thesis as examples of “automatic uprisings”. The thesis’ discussion of a SI poses the

dilemma of “protest or revolution” as a viable ethical problem that contributes to the

[Source page 2]

recent surge of scholarship on AI and democracy that is facilitated in Aarhus at the

SHAPE research centre, and nationwide at ADD (Algorithms, Data and Democracy).


The SI archive is an impossible synthesis of AI advocacy positions. I will collect new

data that provides a hodgepodge of 1) artworks and reports on AI’s harms and social

implications from the materials of AI-bias advocacy groups (Ada Lovelace Institute,

Algorithmic Justice League, AlgorithmWatch, etc.), and 2) diagrams and theories on

AI’s existential risks as developed by AI-safety advocacy groups (Centre for Study of

Existential Risk, MIRI, Future of Humanity Institute, etc.). The aim of such a synthetic

mixture of the “left and right-wing” of AI advocacies is to inject a socio-emancipatory

sensibility into the ultra-rationalist imaginary surrounding the AI takeover. Importantly,

using AI to form the SI as an archive clarifies that I do not actually posit sentient AI in

any present tense.


The project’s main deliveries will be to present SI’s archive by 1) a project website

[████], 2) regular exhibitions within the

upcoming PL<A/T>FORMIZATION digital art space of Aksioma (Slovenija), Kunsthal

Aarhus (Denmark) and POINT (Cyprus), 3) a conclusive ‘total’ installation in the

exhibition space of Kunsthal Aarhus, 4) four artist-led workshops about AI, art and

democracy at ArtHub Copenhagen, Dokk1, Kunsthal Aarhus and KPMG NewTech

arranged with SHAPE and Centre for Aesthetics of AI Images (Aarhus University),

and 5) the publication of 3 articles to form the bulk of the written dissertation: 1) AI &

Society: “An Amalgam of AI Advocacy: Protest or Revolution?“, 2) Études Digitales:

“Doing Contributory and Practice-Based Research after La Société Automatique”, &

3) e-flux: “Automatic Uprisings: Lessons from Archiving a Synthetic International”.
