ARTWORK

Title: Speaking in Public MIT KI
Year: 2026
Medium: mobile installation, electric vehicles, sound system, microphones, LED displays, printed
documents, on-device artificial intelligence interfaces

Speaking in Public MIT KI is a mobile installation staging the political party MIT KI as an apparatus of
public address. Electric vehicles carry loudspeakers, microphones, LED displays, printed documents, and
on-device artificial intelligence interfaces. Together, they form a temporary setting for public speech,
proposals, and responses. The work organizes the technical and spatial conditions that make political
speech audible, visible, and contestable in public space.

The installation functions as a minimal party congress. Visitors, delegates, press, and members of the
public take part in structured conversations on the MIT KI programme. MIT KI is a synthetic political
party preparing to run for mayor in Linz in 2027. There are three festival moments when assemblies
activate a forum for decision-making. Participants formulate proposals and respond to positions generated
by synthetic agents. They also negotiate priorities for the party’s programme and organisational direction.

Sound functions as a medium of political presence. Speeches, statements, and synthetic voices circulate
through the audio system. Legitimacy is tested in the public voicing of positions, others' responses, and
exposure to shared judgment. The installation draws on the formats of campaign vehicles, mobile stages,
and sound system culture. It stages the party as an interface between representation, technical mediation,
and shared authorship. Within this setting, human participants and synthetic agents shape programmes,
positions, and procedures together.


BIOGRAPHY

MIT KI is a synthetic political party started in Linz [████]. The project develops experimental institution-building formats.
It investigates how political organisation arises through interactions between human and synthetic agents.
Working across practice-based research and participatory performance, MIT KI explores presence,
legitimacy, and collective decision-making. These qualities take form through technical interfaces and
rule-based procedures. The project treats the political party as an evolving deliberative infrastructure
rather than a fixed representational structure. Previous formats include KI Dipfel Gipfeltreffen, a four-day
series of speculative rule-play assemblies. These were developed with Computer Lars of the Synthetic
Party in Denmark.
