             AUTOMATIC UPRISINGS

                ARCHIVING A TECHNO-SOCIAL SCULPTURE


Abbreviations

     -    Artificial intelligence: AI

     -  The Synthetic International: SI


Background and purpose


AUTOMATIC UPRISINGS raises a double-edged scenario: what to do if the “robot uprising”

has already happened, and the prophesied explosion of artificial superintelligence only led to

an endless supercharging of bureaucratic stupidity? Its postulate is that this must introduce a

new imperative for politically-engaged artists: namely to employ technologies for a cultural

revolt that targets the abiding futurism of imaginaries related to the 4th Industrial Revolution.


Given that artists must fight bureaucratic stupidity, the radical research question should be:

what new forms of social protest became possible through yesterday’s robot uprising? Here,

artistic practice can take upon a responsibility to subvert all upscaled projections à la Skynet.

It must be recognized how buzzwords of artificial intelligence (AI), disruption and digitisation

pose a threat to democracy that is similar to why global social movements fight capitalism,

climate change and racial injustice. The question is explored by practicing how media-based

and socio/politically-engaged art can challenge algorithmic governmentality (Stiegler 2021).


The purpose of the practice-based project is to create an archive for a potential network that

acts as a representative of contemporary “automatic uprisings” on a global scale. The idea is

to document a techno-social sculpture, The Synthetic International (SI), as a web of political

parties driven by methods of generative AI. As an artwork, the SI will operate between two

layers: 1) while the potential network is a conceptual piece that will largely consist outside of

myself by use of AI, 2) the substantial artistic element is to archive the visual and textual

materials that the SI can evoke through interactions between humans and AI. All material

production will take place within specific techno-social milieus of my design and facilitation.


The SI’s societal role will reenact many of the oppositions inherent to the history of political

art. By oscillating between actuality/potentiality, party/movement, and performance/politics,

its background involves e.g. Joseph Beuys’ social sculpting from The German Student Party

to Die Grünen (1967-1979), the net artists Wiktoria Cukt who ran a virtual political candidate

Hypothesis

The project’s main hypothesis is that practice-based research can develop an immanent

critique of the power that processes of automation already exercise within the bureaucracy

and public spheres in a way that answers critical theory’s recent call to arms for imagining a

genuine democratization of AI and data infrastructures. The thesis argues that diverse

potentialities of automation and data can be enacted by use of archiving as artistic practice.


New important works of critical theory have argued for radical digital democratization (active

participation and collective ownership), as they evaluate that data infrastructures and its

forms of power currently foster an authoritarian centralization of control (McQuillan 2022;

Muldoon 2022; Zuboff 2022). However, critical theory’s largely external perspective makes it

difficult to imagine new alternatives. In contrast, practice-based researchers can use e.g.

generative AI auto-critically (Chatonsky 2022) to make the media a “self”-reflexive resource.


The project’s critical reflection will evolve through a humanities conception of algorithms and

data as acts of archiving (Agostinho et al. 2019). Its point of departure is philosopher Yuk

Hui’s theory of contemporary data-driven archives as a “new synthesis of time” (Hui 2018).

Simply put: Hui posits that archiving is no longer a way of conserving the past to anticipate

the future, but a tool of prediction that makes up the future through the past (also Longo

2022). What this theory of potentiality can offer to a practice-based criticism is to legitimize

how new political imaginaries can be sculpted from the empirical evidence, e.g. by analyzing

generated data.


Methodology

The project’s methodologies can be summarized as an “archivism” by which  it updates a

tradition within avant-garde art for relating bureaucratic pastiche with new media technology

(Spieker 2008). The project is to deploy: 1) experimental methods from creative AI practices

such as data collection, machine learning training, and generative AI, plus contributory

research frameworks such as laboratories and workshops, 2) artistic-visual techniques, e.g.

collages and montages, web design, remediations between analogue-digital media and 3)

theoretical methods of conceptual critique and iconological image analysis.

From 2023-26, the project will collect visual and textual data from advocacy groups on social

bias in AI (Algorithmic Justice League, AlgorithmWatch, etc.), and rework source materials

(chatbot transcripts, interviews and images) that I evoked through the global media artwork

of The Synthetic Party that presented a Danish political party led by an AI chatbot. The SI’s

website will feature AI models that respond to textual questions with imagery - ranging from

AI propaganda to twisted diagrams (Illustration 1-2).


The thesis can analyze how to relate the SI archive with the techno-political superstructures

of data-driven AI (ytiegler, Hui and Longo) by contextualizing methods of visual analysis

such as Panofsky and Warburg's iconological syntheses (Mitchell 1986).


A bridging of creative AI with contributory research constitutes the methodological innovation

of this project in comparison to the few published practice-based projects on art and current

AI. While their concern is how AI affects artistic practice (Amerika 2022), this project rather

asks how practice changes AI (Tromble 2020). In the context of artistic practice, contribution

is a digital studies’ interpretation of Beuysian social sculpting as an inscription of multiplicity

(human, non-human) that frames the hermeneutics of creative research (Stiegler 2021). The

contributory practices will be open labs, workshops and human-AI interaction.


Deliveries and realization

The project’s main deliveries will be:


1) laboratories on “techno-social sculpting” in Kunsthal Aarhus, and a series of workshops in

Denmark and abroad

2) three digital art exhibitions curated by Aksioma (Slovenia), Kunsthal Aarhus (Denmark)

and POINT (Cyprus)

3) three journal articles to form a written thesis:

     -   AI & Society: “An Iconology of AI Advocacy: Protesting AI with AI“

     -   Études Digitales: “Techno-Social Sculpting in the wake of La Société Automatique”

     -   e-flux: “Automatic Uprisings: Lessons from Archiving a Synthetic International”
