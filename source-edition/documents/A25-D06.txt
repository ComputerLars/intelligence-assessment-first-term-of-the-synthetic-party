 Synthetic Summit 2025: Towards AI-driven World Co-Ordination

 “Workings of the world untie! You have a win to world.”
           - Proposed slogan for a Cyborg International, by McKenzie Wark, 2017

In Spring 2025, the art space Kunsthal Aarhus hosts the inaugural "Synthetic
Summit"— an exhibition-event curated by the ‘Computer Lars’-collective,
originating from Denmark's ‘The Synthetic Party’ (Det Syntetiske Parti).

The summit invites the leading AI-driven political parties and virtual politicians
from around the world, herein namely The Australian AI Party, The Finnish AI
Party, The Japanese AI Party, Politician SAM (New Zealand), the Simiyya
platform (Egypt-Jordan), The Swedish AI Party, The Synthetic Party (Denmark),
the Social Democratic chatbot Rosa (Denmark), the robopolitician and world
co-ordinator Stephen Byerley (USA), and Wiktoria Cukt (Poland). Participants
present a collaborative vision for AI-driven planetary governance.

The Synthetic Summit offers a groundbreaking experiment into AI-driven world
coordination on a planet increasingly strained by geopolitical tensions and
rapid advancements in AI technology. Although there has never been a unified
world government on Earth, despite numerous attempts by the modernist
ideologies, the Synthetic Summit suggests that AI-driven global coordination
is poised to and must emerge.

Today, Earth is beleaguered by endless wars in Ukraine, Palestine, Syria,
Congo, Ethiopia, Nagorno-Karabakh, Somalia, Yemen, Libya, Myanmar, and
many others. New conflicts are also emerging in Venezuela, North Korea,
India-Pakistan, and Taiwan. Compounding these issues are ecological
disasters, racial tensions, economic crises, terrorism, famine, cyber attacks,
and disruptions in supply chains. Given that the barely concealed hostilities
among corporations and states continue to create an anarchic system of
international relations, the evolution towards AI-driven governance seems an
almost inevitable next step in the trajectory of global infrastructures.

In this context of pervasive turmoil, the concept of political decision-making
power has already long been compromised and overshadowed by planetary
computation and algorithmic governance systems. While AI regulation today
rallies politicians, business leaders, and lobbyists in international conference
centers, it is clear that humanity’s designated representatives can only operate

within capitalist-modernist frameworks of artificial agents and nation-states.
As Earth moves towards the spread of artificial intelligence on a global scale,
it may be time to abandon the representative governance systems altogether.
This might be a chance to fully recalibrate formal democracy beyond the
constraints of political authority.

The Summit’s scenario of AI-driven planetary governance diverges from the
narratives of singularitarian superintelligence often developed by capitalist
enterprises. Instead, it is rooted in the concept of a "World Co-Ordinator,"
originally envisioned by science fiction writer Isaac Asimov in his works
"Evidence" (1946) and "The Evitable Conflict" (1950). In Asimov’s vision, a
democratically elected robot named Stephen Byerley oversaw a global
machine economy driven through planetary computation, encompassing
co-ordination of regional machines situated in the East, Europe, North, and the
Tropics. Inspired by this framework, the Synthetic Summit seeks to establish a
foundation for how democratically elected AI can collaborate based on their
underlying infrastructural systems of planetary computation.

Unlike traditional assemblies focused on “AI governance”—such as Taiwan's
diverse Alignment Assemblies, the Frontier Model Forum of BigAI (Anthropic,
Microsoft, Google, and OpenAI), the Global AI Safety Summits initiated by the
UK government, and the UN's AI For Good Summits—the "Synthetic Summit"
delves into the concept of AI-driven governance. It will simulate an entirely
synthetic form of assembly, where the primary participants are AI parties and
virtual politicians - envisioning a political modus where every decision-making
process is visibly algorithmic. This contrasts with how “AI governance” seek to
regulate AI technologies by the human-centric systems of democracy and law.

The Synthetic Summit will transform Kunsthal Aarhus into a congress center
where immersive performances and interactive installations will simulate both
parliamentary proceedings and conference negotiations. This event mirrors
and extends the global discourse on AI's societal role, recalibrating the
political and philosophical frameworks of formal democracy. By imagining AI
as not merely a tool under human oversight but a planetary stakeholder in
itself, the summit aims to push the boundaries of AI governance debates.

In conclusion, the “Synthetic Summit 2025” is more than an artistic or political
exercise; it acts as a probe into the future of governance. By transitioning from
traditional, human-centered governance to a model where AI is a competing
agent of political authority, the summit prompts a shift in how to conceive and

6. Poland: Wiktoria Cukt, virtual presidential candidate and political party

- Overview: A project by the Centralny Urząd Kultury Technicznej (C.U.K.T), Wiktoria
Cukt was a virtual presidential candidate launched in 2001. W

Wiktoria Cukt, CUKT’s virtual presidential candidate, was the culmination of the
group's projects that used rave parties. The artists created a candidate who was a
computer program, so-called Citizen Election Software (OSW, Obywatelski
Software Wyborczy). Wiktoria Cukt represented the views of everyone who decided
to choose her as a representative of their views. She demonstrated a faith in direct,
participatory democracy without unnecessary, harmful middlemen, which CUKT
considered politicians to be. Wiktoria Cukt's campaign slogan was: “Politicians are
useless”. Each time a campaign office for Wiktoria Cukt was opened, there was a
rave party. The parties were an occasion to round up Wiktoria's policies – what
people wanted her to say. CUKT members also gathered signatures for her official
candidacy and membership declarations for the Wiktoria Cukt political party.

Creators: The collective C.U.K.T. (Centralny Urząd Kultury Technicznej, or Central
Bureau of Technical Culture), made by Rafał Ewertowski, Mikołaj Jurkowski, Artur
Kozdowski, Jacek Niegoda, Maciej Sienkiewicz, and [████].

Your AI has to tackle an escalating geopolitical conflict that threatens global stability.
