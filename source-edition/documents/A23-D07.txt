             AUTOMATIC UPRISINGS

                ARCHIVING A TECHNO-SOCIAL SCULPTURE


Abbreviations

     -    Artificial intelligence: AI

     -  The Synthetic International: SI

Background


AUTOMATIC UPRISINGS raises a double-edged scenario: what to do if the “robot uprising”

has already happened, and the prophesied explosion of artificial superintelligence only led to

an endless supercharging of bureaucratic stupidity? Its postulate is that this introduces a

new imperative for politically-engaged artists: namely to employ technologies for a cultural

revolt that targets the abiding futurism of imaginaries related to the 4th Industrial Revolution.


Given that artists have a special responsibility for fighting bureaucratic stupidity, the radical

research question should be: what new forms of social protest became possible through

yesterday’s robot uprising? In my practice,  I have utilized advances in generative AI to

construct a vox populi that enacts democratic participation from a distributed pool of

human-made data (Xiang 2022). As such, I have worked to subvert any cultural projection of

technological autonomy à la the doomsday visions of Skynet from the Terminator-franchise.


The challenge is however that social uprisings with AI technology risk a greater complicity

with  Big  Tech’s democratic problems than when  e.g.  global  social movements  fight

capitalism, climate change or racial injustice. This project’s aim is therefore to develop an

immanent critique by reenacting the core dialectical oppositions of political art, such as the

plays between actuality/potentiality, party/movement, and performance/politics in e.g. Joseph

Beuys’ social sculpting from The German Student Party to Die Grünen (1967-1979) or when

a group or net artists ran the virtual political candidate Wiktoria Cukt in Poland (2001).

Purpose


The project’s purpose is to document and theorize a potential socio-political network called

The Synthetic International (SI). It will depict the workings of a global web of political parties

that are driven by AI. These parties are supposed to represent the abstentionist population

of their respective countries through curated datasets. Relating algorithmic representation to

the politically unrepresented constitutes my specific variation of a techno-populist ideology

(Pietrzak 2022; Vukovic 2022).


As a techno-social sculpture vis-à-vis Beuys, the SI can operate between two layers: 1)

while the potential network is a conceptual piece that will largely consist outside of myself by

use of AI, 2) the substantial artistic element is to archive the visual-textual materials that the

SI evokes through interactions between humans and AI. All materials are created within

techno-social milieus of my design and facilitation.

Hypothesis


The project’s main hypothesis is that practice-based research can develop an immanent

critique of the power that processes of automation already exercise within the bureaucracy

and public spheres. In  this way,  it  will answer  critical theory’s recent call to arms for

envisioning a democratization of AI and data infrastructures. The thesis argues that diverse

potentialities of automation and data can be enacted by use of archiving as artistic practice.


Recently, important works of critical theory have argued for radical digital democratization

(active participation and collective ownership), as they evaluate that data infrastructures and

its forms of power are fostering an authoritarian centralization of control (McQuillan 2022;

Muldoon 2022; Zuboff 2022). However, critical theory’s largely external perspective makes it

difficult to imagine new alternatives. In contrast, practice-based researchers can use e.g.

generative AI auto-critically (Chatonsky 2022) to make the media a “self”-reflexive resource.


The project’s critical reflection will evolve through a humanities conception of algorithms and

data through theories of the archive as a field of overlooked potentials (Agostinho et al.

2019). Its point of departure is philosopher Yuk Hui’s idea of data-driven archives as a “new

synthesis of time” (Hui 2018). Simply put: Hui posits that archiving is no longer a way of

conserving the past to anticipate the future, but a tool of prediction that makes up the future

through the past. What Hui’s theory of potentiality can offer to my practice-based criticism is

to legitimize that new political imaginaries can be sculpted from the empirical evidence, e.g.

through generated data.

Methodology


The project’s methodologies can be summarized as an “archivism” by which  it updates a

tradition within avant-garde art for relating bureaucratic pastiche with new media technology

(Spieker 2008). The project is to deploy: 1) experimental methods from creative AI practices

such as data collection, machine learning training, and image generation, plus contributory

research frameworks such as laboratories and workshops, 2) artistic-visual techniques, e.g.

collages and montages, web design, remediations between analogue-digital media and 3)

theoretical methods of conceptual critique and iconological visual analysis.


From 2023-26, I 1) collect visual data from advocacy groups on social bias in AI (Algorithmic

Justice League, AlgorithmWatch, etc.), and 2) rework source materials (chatbot transcripts,

interviews and images) that  I evoked through the global media artwork of The Synthetic

Party on a Danish political party led by an AI chatbot. The SI’s website will feature AI models

that respond to textual questions with imagery - ranging from AI propaganda to twisted

diagrams (Illustration 1-2).


The thesis can analyze how to relate the SI archive with the techno-political superstructures

of data-driven AI by contextualizing methods of visual analysis in the tradition of Panofsky

and Warburg's iconological syntheses (Mitchell 1986). This analysis will compare AI images

with their model’s system card to carve out political visions within a complex of algorithmic

agency and cultural bias.


A bridging of creative AI with contributory research constitutes the methodological innovation

of this project in comparison to the few published practice-based projects on art and current

AI. While their concern is how AI affects artistic practice (Amerika 2022), my project rather

asks how practice changes AI  (e.g. Tromble 2020). In the context of  artistic practice,

contribution here refers to an interpretation of Beuysian social sculpting as a creative

research framework that aims to challenge algorithmic governmentality (Stiegler 2021). The

contributory practices will be open labs, workshops and human-AI interaction.

Deliveries and realization


The project’s main deliveries will be:


1) laboratories on “techno-social sculpting” in Kunsthal Aarhus, and a workshop series in

Denmark and abroad


2) three digital art exhibitions curated by Aksioma (Slovenia), Kunsthal Aarhus (Denmark)

and POINT (Cyprus)


3) three journal articles to form a written thesis:


   ●  AI & Society: “An Iconology of AI Advocacy: Protesting AI with AI“

   ●  Études Digitales: “Techno-Social Sculpting in the wake of La Société Automatique”
