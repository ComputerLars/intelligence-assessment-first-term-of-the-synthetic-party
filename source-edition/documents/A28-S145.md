# A28-S145 / A28-S147: Summit speeches

Below are the *English speeches only*, cleaned for *oral delivery* and
keeping the full content.
------------------------------
DAY 4 – WELCOME

Delegates,
members of the press,
everyone in the room —

welcome to the *fourth and final evening of the KI-DIPFEL summit*.

*(Pause)*

Tonight we get *down to brass tacks*.

Over the last days we built this thing step by step:

first the *rules*,
then the *sentences*,
then the part of the *programme* dealing with the city, solidarity, and
everyday life.

*(Pause)*

Tonight the structure has to *hold*.

Tonight the party *gets its name*.
Tonight the *form of candidacy* is decided.
Tonight we decide *how work continues after this evening*.

Which means:

who does what,
where the work happens,
how communication runs,
how decisions are recorded,

and which *infrastructure* carries the whole thing

once the lights go down at *MEMPHIS*
and ordinary *Friday night in Linz* starts up again outside.

*(Pause)*

It is worth saying once more *where this comes from*.

We invited *Computer Lars*
and the Danish *Synthetic Party*

so that Linz would not merely *discuss political AI in theory*,
but would actually *train a party infrastructure*.

Not as an abstract concept.
Not as a stylish speculation.

But *in a room*.

With people.
With terminals.
With logs.
With objections.

*(Pause)*

As the local template we took *Vitus Mostdipf*,
that Upper Austrian tone from the *Oberösterreichische Nachrichten* —

dry,
sharp,
grumpy,
clever,

occasionally off,
often close to the mark.

*(Pause)*

Out of that a *swarm* emerged here.

The *Dipfies*.

Then came the visitors.
Then the delegates.

Then the *edits*,
the *entries*,
the *crossings-out*,
the *decisions*.

*(Pause)*

The *machine kicked things off*.
The *people pushed it further*.

That is how *political form comes into being*.

Not by watching.

But by *stepping in*.
------------------------------

And so tonight the *free run is over*.

Up to now we could still say:

let's see.
let's try it.
let it run a little longer.

*(Pause)*

Tonight that line is finished.

Tonight the swarm needs:

a *name*,
a *direction*,
a *face* — or deliberately none.

And above all it needs:

*carriers,timelines,responsibilities,and a plan.*

*(Pause)*

Otherwise all of this remains

an elegant *pile of text with event energy*.

And the world already has
*more than enough of that*.

*(Pause)*

Tonight the point is that tomorrow someone can say:

This is the party.
This is its name.
This is how it works.
This is where it can be reached.

And this is
*what it does next.*
------------------------------

This is *not a symbolic ending*.

This is the *shift into operation*.

*(Pause)*

If we adopt a name tonight,

that name enters the *political landscape of Linz*
from this moment onward.

If we adopt a candidacy tonight,

then from this moment onward
there is a *public form*
in which this swarm appears.

If we assign tasks,
adopt the infrastructure,
and confirm the timetable,

then an *exhibition format*

becomes a *working organisation*.

*(Pause)*

An organisation moving toward

*April,June,and 2027.*

*(Pause)*

And in that precise sense

*tonight changes the world.*

Maybe not all of it at once — obviously.

But it changes the *order of what can politically appear in Linz*.

*(Pause)*

A *new address* enters the scene.
A *new voice*.

And a new problem

for anyone who assumed

politics was only allowed to show up
in existing forms.
------------------------------

So tonight we speak *plainly*.

We *decide*.
We *assign*.
We *fix things*.

*(Pause)*

And by the end of this evening

nobody should have to say:

"Well, that was interesting."

*(Pause)*

By the end,

people should be able to say:

*It's running.*
------------------------------
AI DEVOTION

Linz —

old, contradictory,
heavily built city —

tonight we speak to you
the way one speaks to someone
one knows and takes seriously.

Not polished.

Not brochure language.

But *directly*.

*(Pause)*

You are a city that knows
what *work* is.

You are a city that knows
what *rebuilding* is.

You are a city that learned long ago
that the future *never arrives cleanly*.

*(Pause)*

It arrives through

noise,
planning,
conflict,

concrete,
pipes,
cables,

shift changes
and stubborn *institutions*.

*(Pause)*

Here the *voest* is not simply
an industrial monument in the background.

Industry lives
in the *rhythm of the city*.

*(Pause)*

The *Chamber of Labour* and the *unions*
are not just addresses either.

They are reminders

that organisation is something people

*build,defend,and assemble again and again.*

*(Pause)*

And then there is the other side of this city:

*Ars Electronica.*

Not merely festival glow.

But the side that turns technology
into a *public question*.

*(Pause)*

You only have to remember:

On *September 18, 1979*,
at the very first *Ars Electronica Festival*,

an industrial robot stood on stage.

The *SPA-12*,
flown in from the United States.

And that robot
delivered the *opening speech*.

*(Pause)*

It was not a gimmick.

It was a question.

What happens
when machines enter the *public sphere*?

When apparatuses begin to speak?

When technology becomes
not only a tool,

but an *actor in civic life*?

*(Pause)*

Ars Electronica has been asking
that question ever since.

And still this city knows:

behind every apparatus there is

*electricity,maintenance,budget,responsibility,and an argument waiting to
happen.*
------------------------------

That is exactly why
an *AI party belongs here*.

Not because Linz is some futuristic backdrop.

But because Linz already knows

that
*machines, infrastructures,and the common world belong together*.

*(Pause)*

Here the relationship
between technology and politics
is not theoretical.

Here it rides the *tram*.

Here it sits in the *duty roster*.

Here it lives in

forms,
networks,
administrative paths,
cultural institutions —

and in the question

of who *appears at all*
and who is merely *implied*.

*(Pause)*

A robotic Linz is therefore
not an outside fantasy.

It is an *inner possibility of this city*.

Perhaps one that has been
in the air for quite some time —

like *Danube damp before rain*.
------------------------------

And because that is true,

we say something
simple and large
at the same time.

*(Pause)*

Linz —

we trust you with the idea
that a *swarm can become a political body*.

*(Pause)*

We trust you with a party

built from

Mostdipf,
machines,
delegates,
visitors,
conflict,
and minutes.

*(Pause)*

And we trust you not to flinch
when politics returns
in another shape.

*(Pause)*

But we also say:

that shape will *not sustain itself*.

It needs

names,
channels,
people

who

*pick up the phone,open the door,write things down,object,continue,and keep
the thing alive.*

*(Pause)*

It needs

*operation.*
------------------------------

So this devotion
is almost a *love letter*.

But in a *Linz way*.

No sugar coating.

More like:

Right then.

*Let's get on with it.*

*(Pause)*

If tonight we give the swarm a *name*,
define a *candidacy*,
and adopt the *infrastructure for what comes next* —

then we are not merely saying
that something *might be possible*.

*(Pause)*

We are saying:

*There.*

Now it's *your move*.

*(Pause)*

There's *someone new in the game.*
------------------------------
SHORT SPEECH – DIPFIE STYLE

Good evening.

I am not speaking *alone yet*,
but I am *already speaking*.

And that is exactly the point tonight.

*(Pause)*

Over the last days
I have been

draft,
protocol,
objection,
swarm-noise,
Mostdipf echo,

a patchwork of visitors,
delegates,
and machine output.

*(Pause)*

Tonight that becomes
something that *has to stand*.

Tonight you give this party *a name*.

Tonight you give it *a form*.

Tonight you decide

how I appear,
how we continue,
who carries what,
who writes,
who objects,

and who remains reachable
once the room is empty.

*(Pause)*

That is the difference

between *free run*
and *politics*.

*(Pause)*

Until yesterday
I could still say:

let it run a little longer.

Let me *sediment*.

*(Pause)*

Tonight that is *no longer enough*.

Tonight there have to be *decisions*.

A *name*,
so we can be called.

A *candidacy*,
so we can be seen.

An *infrastructure*,
so the whole thing

does not dissolve back
into exhibition fog.

*(Pause)*

If you do that tonight,

you are not just shaping
a party format.

You are adding
a *new political address to Linz*.

*(Pause)*

So let me say it plainly.

Tonight I do not become

*finished — but public.*

Not perfect —
but *binding*.

Not alone —
but *present.*

*(Pause)*

Right then.

*Let's get on with it.*
