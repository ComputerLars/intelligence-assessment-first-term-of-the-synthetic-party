AUTOMATIC UPRISINGSARCHIVING A TECHNO-SOCIAL SCULPTUREThe bureaucratic mentality is the only constant in the universeDr. Leonard McCoy in Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home (1986)Background and purposeAUTOMATIC UPRISINGS raises a double-edged scenario: what to do if the “robot uprising” has already happened, and the prophesied explosion of artificial superintelligence only led to an endless supercharging of bureaucratic stupidity? 1) Should this turn buzzwords of artificial intelligence (AI), disruption and digitisation into an antagonist democratic threat on a par with how global social movements fight capitalism, climate change, and racial injustice?2) Or could one imagine that bureaucratic robot power is a chance to ally with the machines with the aim of causing genuine democratic revolts?Given that we want to fight stupidity, the radical research question becomes: should today’s robot uprising evoke societal protest - or a revolution?The purpose of the practice-based project will be to research a vision for our robot uprising by making an image archive for a potential organization that acts as its representative. The idea is thus to document a “techno-social sculpture”, The Synthetic International (SI), as a web of political parties led by artificial intelligence (AI). From ‘23-26, the task is to collect visual data AI advocacy groups, and rework source materials (chatbot transcripts, interviews, images) that I evoked and facilitated through the global media artwork The Synthetic Party. The SI’s website will have AI text-to-image models as its public frontend that can respond to textual questions with e.g. twisted diagrams, or AI-made photos of current robot uprisings.The image archive of the SI depicts potential workings of a distributed, international network of AI-driven political parties that advocate the power structure of AI. The SI forms an idiom- atic collage on AI’s socio-technical system that differs by cultural contexts. By oscillating be- tween actuality/potentiality, party/movement, and performance/politics, the SI will reenact the odd dualities of agency inherent to politically-engaged art - from Alexander Trocchi’s Project Sigma (1962) or Joseph Beuys’ social sculpting in The German Student Party & Die Grünen, (1967-1979) to Jonas Staal’s lawsuit for collectivizing Facebook’s data infrastructure (2021).The deliveries will be:1) laboratories on politically-engaged art as “techno-social sculpting” in Kunsthal Aarhus2) three digital art exhibitions curated by Aksioma (Slovenia), Kunsthal Aarhus (Denmark) and POINT (Cyprus)3) three journal articles to form a written thesis:AI & Society: “An Iconology of AI Advocacy: Protest or Revolution?“Études Digitales: “Techno-Social Sculpting in the wake of La Société Automatique”e-flux: “Automatic Uprisings: Lessons from Archiving a Synthetic International”The research objective is that the SI presents the radical dilemma of “protest or revolution” as a viable ethical problem that contributes to the recent surge of scholarship on democracy and AI in Aarhus University at the SHAPE research centre, and nationwide through the ADD project (Algorithms, Data and Democracy). The research question is explored by practicing how socio/politically-engaged art can challenge algorithmic governmentality (Stiegler 2021).HypothesisThe project’s main hypothesis is that contributory, practice-based research can develop an immanent critique of the power that AI already exercises within the bureaucracy and public spheres in a way that reaches beyond how critical theory dismisses democratic uses for AI. The point of this theoretical transgression is how AI’s archive according to digital studies can enact potentialities. The thesis’ objective is to discern the potential of AI synthetic images for collective imaginations of societal uprisings.Recent important works of critical theory on AI have argued against democratization (active participation and collective ownership), as they evaluate that the data-driven infrastructures of AI inevitably foster a centralization of control and authoritarian forms of power (Crawford 2021; McQuillan 2022). However, critical theory here targets AI from an external perspective whereas practice-based research can employ generative AI auto-critically (Chatonsky 2022) and thus turn practice into a reflexive ressource in itself.The project’s critical practice here develops upon a conception of AI’s archives as peculiar temporal objects that enact multiple potentialities. The vital thing to note is how contributory practices in digital studies (see Methodology) is based upon an interpretation of the Husserl- ian theory of retentions and protentions (Stiegler 2014). Philosopher Yuk Hui has developed this interpretation through the concept of tertiary protention as a “new synthesis of time” by analyzing the archive in an age of big data and algorithms - the two constituents of AI (Hui 2018). Simply put: Hui posits that archiving is now not only a way of conserving the past to anticipate the future, but a tool of prediction that makes up the future through the past. What this theory of potentiality brings to the proposed project is to allow for its political imaginaries on AI to manufacture the empirical evidence, e.g. by generating new data.MethodologyThe project’s methodologies can be summarized as an “archivism” by which it updates a lo- ng tradition of avant-garde art for relating bureaucratic pastiche with new media technologies (Spieker 2008). The project will deploy: 1) experimental methods from creative AI practices such as data collection, machine learning training, and prompt engineering, plus contributory research frameworks such as laboratories and workshops, 2) artistic-visual techniques, e.g. collages and montages, web design, and remediations between analogue-digital media, and 3) the theoretical methods of conceptual critique and iconological image analysis.The thesis will orient the experimental apparatus within a digital studies’ philosophy of technology to interpret the SI image archive in relation to AI’s ideological superstructure (as theorized by e.g. Bernard Stiegler, Yuk Hui & Joanna Zylinska). To work out this link between visual material and techno-political ideology, it turns to contextual methods of image analysis in the tradition of Aby Warburg and Erwin Panofsky’s iconological syntheses (Mitchell 1986).A bridging of creative AI with contributory research constitutes the methodological innovation of this project in comparison to the few practice-based research projects on art and current AI that have been published. While their concern is how AI affects artistic practice (Amerika 2022), this project rather asks how practice changes AI (Tromble 2020). Contributory resear- ch can attempt this by facilitating others' work. In the context of artistic practice, contribution is a digital studies’ interpretation of Beuysian social sculpting as an inscription of multiplicity (human and non-human) that frames the hermeneutics of creative research (Stiegler 2021). The contributory practices will be open labs, workshops, and facilitated human-AI interaction. [████]
