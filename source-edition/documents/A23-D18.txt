AUTOMATIC UPRISINGSARCHIVING A TECHNO-SOCIAL SCULPTUREThe bureaucratic mentality is the only constant in the universeDr. Leonard McCoy in Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home (1986)Background and purposeAUTOMATIC UPRISINGS raises a double-edged scenario: what to do if the “robot uprising” has already happened, and the prophesied explosion of artificial superintelligence only led to an endless supercharging of bureaucratic stupidity? The postulate is that this must introduce a new imperative for politically-engaged artists: namely to employ technologies for a form of protest that unlearns the abiding cultural imaginaries related to the 4th Industrial Revolution. Because if the robots have risen to power, then counterpower should be standing with them.Given that artists must fight bureaucratic stupidity, the radical research question should be: what vision of societal protest became possible through today’s robot uprising? Here, artistic practice can take upon a responsibility to subvert any futurist projections (that art might itself have evoked) by recognising buzzwords of artificial intelligence, disruption and digitisation as an antagonist democratic threat on a par with how global social movements fight capitalism, climate change, or racial injustice.The purpose of the practice-based project is to create an archive for a potential organization that acts as representative for a contemporary “automatic uprising”. The idea is to document a techno-social sculpture, The Synthetic International (SI), as a web of political parties driven by artificial intelligence (AI). From ‘23-26, the task will be to collect textual and visual data from existing AI advocacy groups (Ada Lovelace Institute, Algorithmic Justice League, AlgorithmWatch, etc.), and rework source materials (chatbot transcripts, interviews, images) that I have evoked and facilitated through the global media artwork The Synthetic Party. The SI’s website will have AI models as its public frontend that respond to textual questions with imagery - ranging from photos of current robot uprisings to twisted diagrams.The SI’s immanent critique will reenact the odd oppositions inherent to the history of political art. Oscillating between actuality/potentiality, party/movement and performance/politics, its concept draws upon e.g. Alexander Trocchi’s cognitariat scheming in Project Sigma (1962), Joseph Beuys’ social sculpting from The German Student Party to Die Grünen (1967-1979) and Jonas Staal’s lawsuit for collectivizing Facebook’s data infrastructure (2021).The deliveries will be:1) laboratories on politically-engaged art as “techno-social sculpting” in Kunsthal Aarhus2) three digital art exhibitions curated by Aksioma (Slovenia), Kunsthal Aarhus (Denmark) and POINT (Cyprus)3) three journal articles to form a written thesis:AI & Society: “An Iconology of AI Advocacy: Protest or Revolution?“Études Digitales: “Techno-Social Sculpting in the wake of La Société Automatique”e-flux: “Automatic Uprisings: Lessons from Archiving a Synthetic International”The research objective is that the SI presents the radical dilemma of protest with/against AI as a viable ethical problem that contributes to the recent surge of scholarship on democracy and AI in Aarhus University at the SHAPE research centre, and nationwide through the ADD project (Algorithms, Data and Democracy). The research question is explored by practicing how socio/politically-engaged art can challenge algorithmic governmentality (Stiegler 2021).HypothesisThe project’s main hypothesis is that contributory, practice-based research can develop an immanent critique of the power that AI already exercises within the bureaucracy and public spheres in a way that reaches beyond how critical theory dismisses democratic uses for AI. The point of this theoretical transgression is how AI’s archive according to digital studies can enact potentialities.Recent important works of critical theory on AI have argued against democratization (active participation and collective ownership), as they evaluate that the data-driven infrastructures of AI inevitably foster a centralization of control and authoritarian forms of power (Crawford 2021; McQuillan 2022). However, critical theory here targets AI from an external perspective whereas practice-based research can employ generative AI auto-critically (Chatonsky 2022) and thus turn practice into a reflexive ressource in itself.The project’s critical practice here develops upon a conception of AI’s archives as peculiar temporal objects that enact multiple potentialities. The vital thing to note is how contributory practices in digital studies (see Methodology) is based upon an interpretation of the Husserlian theory of retentions and protentions (Stiegler 2014). Philosopher Yuk Hui has developed this theory through the concept of tertiary protention as a “new synthesis of time” by analyzing the archive in an age of big data and algorithms - the two constituents of AI (Hui 2018). Simply put: Hui posits that archiving is now not only a way of conserving the past to anticipate the future, but a tool of prediction that makes up the future through the past. What Hui’s theory of potentiality brings to the proposed project is to allow for political visions for AI to manufacture the empirical evidence, e.g. by generating new data.MethodologyThe project’s methodologies can be summarized as an “archivism” by which it updates a long tradition of avant-garde art for relating bureaucratic pastiche with new media technologies (Spieker 2008). The project will deploy: 1) experimental methods from creative AI practices such as data collection, machine learning training, and prompt engineering, plus contributory research frameworks such as laboratories and workshops, 2) artistic-visual techniques, e.g. collages and montages, web design, remediations between analogue-digital media and 3) theoretical methods of conceptual critique and iconological image analysis.The thesis will orient the experimental apparatus within a digital studies’ philosophy of technology to interpret SI’s text-to-image archive in relation to AI’s ideological superstructure (as theorized by e.g. Bernard Stiegler, Yuk Hui or Joanna Zylinska). To work out these links between symbolic material and techno-political ideology, it will turn to contextualizing visual analysis such as the iconological syntheses following Panofsky and Warburg (Mitchell 1986).A bridging of creative AI with contributory research constitutes the methodological innovation of this project in comparison to the few practice-based research projects on art and current AI that have been published. While their concern is how AI affects artistic practice (Amerika 2022), this project rather questions how practice changes AI (Tromble 2020). Contributory research can attempt this by facilitating others' work. In the context of artistic practice, contribution is a digital studies’ interpretation of Beuysian social sculpting as an inscription of multiplicity (human and non-human) that frames the hermeneutics of creative research (Stiegler 2021). The contributory practices will be facilitated through open labs, workshops, and human-AI interaction. [████]
