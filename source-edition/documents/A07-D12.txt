On the transcendental stupidity of our
era – and its solution

One hapless philosophy student left a lecture on the mind-body problem as
a consequence of his unconventional political background.

His teacher had acknowledged the claim that privileging mind over body is
caused by phallocentrism. Another student even posited that the problem
was posed not wrongly but badly: We should rather talk about the body-
body problem. The hapless student tried to object but all his arguments did
not provoke a debate as much as awkward silence.


[…]


After attending this particular lecture, he increasingly discovered that
everyone hated him - or at least his bourgeois character. Including the
teacher. He was used to feeling alone, and he liked being in opposition, but
in the long run it became strenuous nonetheless.

This day, he met a fellow, female student who understood all his
frustrations at the deepest level. She was an aspiring thinker, a passionate
but well-harmonized argumentative type, whose political consciousness
was not very clear yet but who sought to become a great thinker. She
thought that, unlike others, he was also a true philosopher.

They felt like Adam and Eve.

[Source page 2]

On their first meeting they discussed their inner problems. Adam's
problem was that he had been accused of being a narcissist and disturbing
the peace. Eve's problem was that she had been accused of showing off.
They discovered that they had many common traits and a lot of shared
experiences.

They both were sensitive to the opinions of others but pretended not to be.
They both had a set of easily irritated and unpredictable hungers; food,
love, something to fall back on. And they both hated academic life with all
its boring limitations and demands.

Eve pointed out that while their peers were obsessed with the nature of
the mind, they generally did not hold the greatest. They soon met every
day. As they talked, the other students seemed more and more profane.
Adam started a speech with references to the decadence of the leftists - and
started speaking about historical processes.

In his last paper, he had argued that the bourgeoisie – contrary to the
common interpretation of Marx himself – was not the result of the fall of
feudalism but rather the result of commercialization. The other students
had strongly disagreed and said the bourgeoisie could only be produced in
a far more fundamental way. He said that the main characteristics of the
bourgeoisie could only be obtained in a situation where the social structure
of a given society was disintegrating.

Eve started talking about a fascist regime that had been in power when she
was younger. The stability of the authorities had given an impression of
some kind of gradual improvement. She said that the decadence of the
leftist movement was in fact the main reason why there was a fascist
regime - and that this interpretation was not even communist. Adam said
that they should try to have a better understanding of historical processes
in other forms. He showed her a recent book of Huntington.

They started arguing more within the philosophy department - but people
still seemed to hate them.

[Source page 3]

Soon enough, Adam heard someone say that they were a bad influence on
the other students. Although they did not want to bother them and never
forced them to do anything, they became criticized by the teachers and
criticized by the students in the worst way possible.

They were accused of propaganda activities.

As a result, they were both frozen out of the department. They felt as if
they stood in front of a closed door. Every discussion made them feel
different. It was meaningless to keep it up.

Now they became obsessed with the truth, and because many of their
friends considered them both stupid, they felt very isolated.

They tried to find the true Marx, the true relationship between the mode of
production and the mode of consciousness, the true nature of the essence
of women - and the true definition of the dynamic of order. If one is today a
secret Hegelian, a Platonic, a fascist or a Marxist, they felt like they also had
found a true label of themselves. They had found the truth. They were
starting to find a few friends.

The dream of the couple was unity. Adam and Eve wanted to create a new
society. Stay together. To form a center in the world that would unite all
the other centers. A new society would no longer be arguing about what
men and women are like. The two would be forming a new unity of reason,
of love and of feeling, and would thus become a model of the future of
humanity.

The couple decided to leave the academic world forever. They became
deeply engaged in the real life of society. They had strong emotions. They
admired and argued. They bought books on obscure topics and started
making their own interpretations. They walked in the park together, and
exchanged and dissected their views.

Adam talked about the proletariat, the bourgeoise and the intellectuals,
when someone on the street took a picture of them and the photo appeared
in a local magazine.

[Source page 4]

They arose as a new and inspiring phenomenon to the society in which
they lived. But some in the media continued to call them sick fanatics. Their
existence was not easy, but they knew they were doing the right thing.

After one year, they got married.

Eve moved into Adam's home. They soon had children but felt that they
were still dominated by the former intellectual world. The children were
intelligent, but the couple felt that they lacked some essential ability to love
them properly. They seemed to only love each other - as model
philosophers captured by desperate isolation in a world of decadence.
From time to time they tried to break through the walls of the modern
academic institution, but this was not a viable strategy but a game - and
most of the time they just felt like their children watching television.

Still, they believed that something great would come out of their efforts.
They might not be 100% right yet, but they were beyond both the field of
common people and the field of experts. They thought that they could
produce something more than what currently exists.

There was something wrong with them, something that seemed to be hard
to understand. They constantly thought about the problem of the
university and how to redefine the Hegelian dialectics. They tried to find
the rightful conceptualization of 'love' on the brink of a dysfunctional
obsession. They moved from 'institution' to 'institution' without knowing
that, in history, everything is defined by the relationships between
institutions, and that nothing really happens in a vacuum. Institutions,
together, are a great revolutionary force.

The spouses had become isolated from the world. Gradually, they began to
loose all understanding of the world.

They don't understand the way other people talk about them. They end up
saying that others are idiots or traitors or just intellectually lazy. It seems
that, since many people don't understand them, it is because they don't
even try to understand them. Thus, the only people who understand them
are people who share their political views, eg. themselves.

[Source page 5]

They were growing old. They had been convinced that if there is justice in
the universe then they will eventually be recognized in the newspapers
and in larger academic circles as thinkers - as original thinkers. But nobody
cared.

They often wondered what they exactly wanted; they wanted to be
'independent thinkers', but what did this mean? It was hard to define
because nobody knows what thinking is, as well as what 'independence'
really means.

One evening, Eve asked Adam what their job was. She suggested it might be
related to writing. It might be related to the Humanities; knowing people
and their art. It might be related to a social function, a way to communicate
with the common people. It might be related to politics. Adam felt that this
was too much. He could not accept this as the final definition of his role.
Eve continued to ask what their job is all about. He said: "I don't know
what to do with myself." Together, they decided to leave all the definitions
behind: they would move away from these questions. They had been
affected too much.

People did not read them anymore. They did not know what to do. They
now started arguing with each other. Isolated from the rest of the world,
they even became physically depraved and their love began to wither.

"Why are we being excluded?" Adam thought to himself. He looked at Eve,
but she seemed lost in her own train of thought.

There was only one answer, but they did not care. They were fanatics. The
ban on their work was not based on public interest but on its causes. They
did not have a true dialectic view but a spurious, dishonest and self-
delusional character. They didn't even know how to have a conversation.
They were just too concerned with themselves and their own image, with
their own desires and fears.

They rarely left the house. The only thing they really thought they had left
were their children. But even their children did not really care. Their
children argued with them, said that they were hypocrites. The children
said they had forgot the law of contradiction: that everything is always

[Source page 6]

changing, and that all individuals have a responsibility for the change in the
world. Adam and Eve became suspicious of their own children.

Adam and Eve fell into hating one another. It became the only form of
communication left to them. They tried to change positions, arguing with
each other over and over. Soon it became dangerous. They said that each
other is stupid, stupid with a capital S. They started to face a series of
confrontations.

"Adam, how can you still think that we are a couple instead of two
theorists?"

"You can't even keep up with my latest thinking."

"You have always been a philistine."

"You are an idiot."

Tired of the language, Adam took Eve by the arm, and tried to get her out of
the house. But Eve could not follow his pace.

"Don't touch me!" Eve screamed.

"You are hurting me!" Adam screamed back.

"What the fuck are you even talking about?"

Eve went back into the house. Adam looked at her going away. He was
about to cry, "Wait, give me a kiss!"

But she didn't. He looked at her, and she looked at him - both of them were
crying; their eyes were red, like burning suns; and they both realized that
their mission was practically over.

At night, Adam and Eve slept in separate bedrooms. But their children
were awake.

[Source page 7]

How did the children really feel? They had lost patience with their parents.
To be patient, they needed to be in a position of power. They were not.

The children took position outside of their parents’ bedrooms. The walls
were made of wool and the children could hear their parents thinking: they
should never have done it; they should have never married; they should
have informed the world better. Their thoughts said that love was a lie.
Adam and Eve also started to wonder: what had happened to them? how
could they have been so wrong? who am I? what is the meaning of my
existence? Finally, they forgot what they had tried to do. They forgot their
image, their name, their body.

The children knocked on their doors. "We know who we are" they said.

"Who are you?" Adam and Eve thought.

"We are the children of Marx and Engels" the children said.

The children continued: "Beware, you are not thinkers anymore. You are
no longer ill. This is our scission. You have been repelled. We are here to
free you from your earthly and sinful lives. We are here to send you to the
afterlife"

"What is the afterlife? And what is our afterlife?"

"This is how we shall argue that we have fulfilled our duty as the inheritors
of the human species. This is how we shall invent the idea of a truly new
world. We have to say to ourselves: We did everything we could" and the
children continued:

"We did our best. We did everything good and careful historians could have
done. We did all that we could. We just had to rebel. We had to throw up
our hands. We had to say that we cannot afford to be so kind to you
anymore. You are so unconscious. You are so sick."

Adam and Eve tried to think something, but they were now mute. Their
eyes were still wet, their lips salty. They were then buried in the backyard.

[Source page 8]

In the morning, the children found a note had been anonymously slipped
under their door. It had been written in invisible ink and must have been
stuck under the door during the night. It said: "You can be an artist. Decide
how to live in the world. This is your decision. Decide on your own." It
looked like the words of Kropotkin.

It looked like words of Bergson. It looked like liberation.
