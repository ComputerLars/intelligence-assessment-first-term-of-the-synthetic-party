The Synthetic Party raises a double-edged scenario: what to do if the “robot uprising” has already happened, and the prophesied explosion of artificial superintelligence only led to an endless supercharging of bureaucratic stupidity? 1) Should this turn the buzzwords of artificial intelligence, disruption and digitisation into an antagonist democratic threat on a par with how global social movements fight capitalism, climate change, and racial injustice? 2) Or could one imagine that bureaucratic robot power is a chance to ally with the machines with the aim of causing a genuine democratic revolt?As thus, the The Synthetic Party evokes an ethical dilemma: should a societal robot uprising evoke societal protest - or a revolution? That is: Can today’s society actually change technology, or should people and technology first set out to remake society?The Synthetic Party addresses the challenges of parliament politics in an age of artificial intelligence and digitisation. Given that bureaucracies and public spheres are to a large extent algorithmically governed, it seems unlikely that the human politicians of today can do anymore than attempting to regulate Big Tech - which probably would only work by collectivising their assets and calling off capitalist economics. What can the digital citizenship of common people set out to change the role of artificial intelligence in modern governance?
