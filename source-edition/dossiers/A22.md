Jeg skriver lige for at høre, om I stadig er interesserede i GPT-3 og kritiske projekter omkring store sprogmodeller.

I stedet for en PhD om emnet, så er jeg blevet ansat som kunstnerisk forsker i tech-firmaet MindFuture. På næste onsdag skal jeg præsentere mit projekt til firmaets offentlige lancering (www.mindfuture.com - jeg hedder Computer Lars, et anagram over Marcel Proust). Det er blevet et ret sært møde mellem IT-branchen, jysk avantgardisme og kunstnerisk forskning omkring AI.

Projektet er et reelt, AI-drevent parti - Det Syntetiske Parti. Det er bygget med GPT3 fra OpenAI og GPT-neo-20b fra EleutherAI, og man kan diskutere med partiets chatbot under ‘Diskussion’ inde på denne side: www.detsyntetiskeparti.org.

Partiet er trænet på data fra alle ikke-opstillingsberettigede partier i Danmark, og er dermed en syntetiseret stemme for hr.hvemsomhelst, sofavælgerne og hverdagsmetafysikerne (lidt i tråd med Søren Mau’s Stem Ikke-projekt). Dertil er det en fremhævelse af spam bots, troldehære og diskrete algoritmers politiske indflydelse, der flytter ansvaret for deres adfærd fra Big Techs regulativer til en demokratisk og juridisk ramme. 

Historisk set er det et forsøg på at realisere den antipolitiske kritik af automatiseringen af samfundet og bureaukratiseringen af kulturen, som i DK sammenhæng bedst er kendt ved Sammenslutningen for Bevidst Arbejdssky Elementer og Jacob Haugaard. Partiet er antipolitisk for at komme rundt om OpenAI’s stramme regler for politisk indhold (der på mange måder mest forhindrer, at man kan undersøge deres politiske impact), og det er avantgardistisk ved at udfolde mere afpersonaliserede organisationsformer for revolutionær virksomhed.

Der er en større samling af AI-generet tekst og billeder med partiet her: medium.com/det-syntetiske-parti

Hvis I er med på at lave noget, så kunne evt. ideer være: en reportage, et interview med partiets chatbot og kunstneren, en web-art anmeldelse, eller jeg kunne sende en kronik til jer.

Jeg vil gerne have noget udfordring og diskussion om kunstprojektet, så jeg kan udvikle AI’en gennem mere aktions- og interventionsbaserede strategier i offentligheden. Og for at få sat lidt kritisk fokus på dansk IT-branche, nu hvor jeg har infiltreret den (og dermed bevidst har inkrimineret projektet…)

statsstyrede portal.

diskussioner med medlemmerne døgnet rundt. I gruppen kan man blandt andet
møde frontfiguren *Leder Lars*, partisekretæren *Computer Lars* samt de
øvrige medlemmer i basisstrukturen *Det Syntetiske Parti*.

Here are the answers. Please write/call if anything.

   1.

   You told me earlier that the project is serious. That it is a serious
   political party, but also an art project. Can you tell me exactly how this
   project developped in your mind and then as a reality and what it is you're
   trying to achieve with it ?

The Synthetic Party is indeed a serious project (meaning committed to
change) but it does not have the same ambitions as a conventional
organization. Although it would be interesting for the party to hold a seat
in parlament, the primary focus has been to evoke a spectacle that could
make people reflect upon the expectations to, and agency of, AI within art,
politics and the technological development of society/the public sphere in
general.

Originally, the idea of an AI-driven party arose from my residency as an
artist-researcher at the tech-hub MindFuture. In my Independent life, I’m a
part of various art collectives such as Computer Lars (computerlars.com),
Arkivaristerne (arkivaristerne.org) and the Organ for Autonomous Sciences
(see eg
https://www.e-flux.com/journal/129/486513/the-resurrection-of-nashism-report-on-the-emergent-forms-of-spectacular-fascism-in-scandinavia/
). So, MindFuture had the idea of introducing “Life with Artificials” as
the 18th SDG in the UN. They wanted to evoke a “global debate” surrounding
our “life with artificial intelligence” through “art” and “provocations”. I
wanted to answer this call through my own artistic research. I asked myself
how to realise this task by using as few resources as possible, and within
a contextual framework that was both artistically and philosophically solid.

I was hired by MindFuture while preparing a collective art exhibition on
the anagrams “Marcel Proust / Computer Lars” (see computerlars.com). For
our purposes, it made sense to explore a company filled with ordinary
people, because we found it difficult to analyse the name of  “Lars” in
itself - that is, without the written corpus of Marcel Proust.

In Denmark, “Lars” is the name of power - (like John in English, or
Francois/Jacques…). There are more CEOs named Lars than there are female
CEOs. Leaders of culture, government, IT, etc. are named Lars. I is the
emblem of the middle-aged white male who is the normal form of subjectivity
in Denmark - the most powerful yet most boring and invisible demographic
group in Denmark. So, we were interested in personifying the abstract
notion of “artificial intelligence” in the name of “Lars”. This is NOT an
anthropomorphisation of AI, but an identification of the human as the force
of production. It made sense to let Lars loose.

As an artist-researcher trained in philosophy, I found that the idea of an
AI-driven party (or a party led by a chatbot named Lars) poses 3 central
challenges for a society of the spectacle:

   -

   It is qualitatively different from examples of “virtual politicians”
   that is based on the appeal of avatars. In contrast, an AI-driven party
   mirrors the discourse of politics as bureaucracy. Here, “Lars” is not an
   avatar as much as a problem of personification.
   -

   It is a direct way to inscribe artificial intelligence in democracy - to
   democratize our data, or to make the data response-able: Who does the data
   represent? (key: Lars…). We provide a way to make technology response-able …
   -

   The concept of a synthetic party can remix the categories “party” and
   “movement”, as it concentrates popular discourse within a statistical
   model. An AI is akin to a party, or any other form of collective
   organisation, as it expresses itself as a distributed entity). As such, it
   concretises radical avant-garde visions like Tiqqun’s The Imaginary
   Party, and actualises theories of the party (such as Lenin’s) within
   techno-capitalism.

   1.

   How has it been received in Denmark? Are people taking it seriously ? Is
   there any chance you'll be on the ballot ? And for example, have you had
   people wanting to join the party, or contribute to Leader Lars' AI ? The
   idea with this question is for me is to understand the story of you
   watching this project go viral, and your reflections on the whole success
   and virality of this thing, and what it says, in your opinion, about our
   conception of AI as a society.

People are taking it more seriously as they get used to it, I think. It is
“sinking in”. But we’re being grouped within the field of art and culture
more than the political news. We have successfully organized some voting
rallies where regular people showed up, and we have also participated in
public AI/human debates with prominent politicians such as city mayors.

The “viral” dimension reflects the media’s framing of AI as a global trend
within a progressive and humanist narrative of technological development.
The media have to write about it. This is of course the necessary logic of
attention that is captured by our concept. I seize this as an opportunity
to disseminate an incredibly complicated story about how “artificial
stupidity” has (probably accidentally) reconfigured the power relations of
posthuman data democracy.

   1.

   Can you tell me how you've made the chatbot ? Cause I have a large
   section of the article about LaMDA, the Google chatbot that made one
   engineer lose his mind. Of course your chatbot cannot be as sophisticated
   as Google's... but what have you discovered about chatbots while making
   one, especially when it comes to politics?

The chatbot is based on GPT-Neo 20xb by EleutherAI. This is the largest
open-source model. We were not allowed to make the project with GPT-3, as
[…] 
Cambridge Analytica, but OpenAI do not possess any power of judgement). We
have finetuned the Eleuther-AI-model with data from fringe parties.

The data has been collected manually. It would not make sense to web
scrape, as one needs a high-quality dataset that is oriented towards
content and style. Also, it does not make sense to input all sorts of
racist tendencies in the chatbot, as there is already a lot of that in the
original dataset. The stuff of interest that AI can learn from fringe
parties is a reflection on what it means to be a micro-political party that
wants to be a part of a Danish parliament.

Actually, it is not only the chatbot that is programmed with fringe
parties. There is also a text generator. There is a big difference between
conversational AI and text generation, but most journalists find it
difficult to explicate that there are always many AIs at play, so this has
been difficult to communicate. So, the “fringe party AI” is in charge of
our policy development, which is published to
https://medium.com/@ComputerLars. The chatbot is a restrained model that
have been prompted. The chatbot sets the day-to-day political course, and
the text generator develops our structure and proposals. They are very
different to engage with.

An interesting thing is actually that nearly all chatbots sound like fringe
parties, because they always express themselves as an amalgam of the
discourse produced by common people.

On a more general note, we want ot do something different than contemporary
art praxis' which uses AI, as they make the interface and the functionality
into the primary focus. In contrast to this, our interest is on the
social/cultural/political-level. Rather than spending time on technological
finesse, it has been more important to represent the ideas in the media and
expand the spectacle of the synthetic party, implementing it in the public
sphere. This is the reason why Leader Lars cannot be an avatar with a face,
gestures and other characteristics of a 'real' politician. Our main
interest has been on the level of the expectations for AI and its social
implementation. This is not very technically demanding, because the
redundancy of the chatbot simply becomes a convincing mirror of the
real-life politician’s administrative redundancy.

   1.

   What do you mean when you say you've programmed it with « fringe party

The parties are all those that have not entered parliament from 1970-2022.
They are super fringe - look at the current list
https://www.vaelgererklaering.dk/partierne. ADHD party, Vodka
Party, etc. It is about the DISCOURSE of the UNREPRESENTED - NOT the
opinions (as they invariably will get mashed up by the AI).

   1.

   One area of interest to me is to see the pitfalls in most of the steps

The 20% is a postulate about an ideal democratic setting. If people do not
vote, it should ideally be because their political view is not represented
by the parties on the ballot. In that case, these people would make their
own political party to promote their point of view. This logical line of
reasoning is very AI…

   1.

   The horizontal line of the graph would go from « AI will be bad » to « AI
   will be good ». And the vertical line goes from « AI is powerless » to « AI
   will be more powerful than humans ». Where would you be on this graph, and
   why ?

We would answer that AI is currently very powerful and is mostly being used
for bad things such as exploitation, discrimination and surveillance. These
futuristic graphs isolate technological development from society at large,
and that is just completely idiotic. The technologies of artificial
intelligence are nothing more than what humans desire them to be.

   1.

   humans overshadow all of the activist work we should be doing about what
   AIs are actually doing now, which they say is detrimental for workers and

Yes, this is true. This is precisely why we inscribe that narrative within
concrete activist work. Because AI has already overpowered humans. However,
“AI” did not do so through cunning intelligence. It was merely an
unintended side effect of the automatised exchange of data for capital.

I have read a lot about your difficult election, and I hope that my answers can help the Brazilians with regaining some sort of new belief in democracy. These are tough times, but also promising and generally unfathomable.

So, I have tried to answer each question as concisely and directly as possible. You’re of course welcome to write me if you have any questions.

All the best, [████], on behalf of Computer Lars and The Synthetic Party

1. Who are you, [████]? What do you do? How does the technology you developed work?

I’m an artist and philosopher appointed at the tech-hub MindFuture. Also, I am representing the art collective “Computer Lars” when I speak about The Synthetic Party, as Computer Lars serves as party secretary (where Leader Lars is our figurehead chatbot).

Our artificial intelligence system consists of a large language model that have been finetuned with data of all “fringe parties” from Denmark since 1970. That includes data from the whole political spectrum. The idea is that this technique can represent the 20% that does not vote in the Danish elections, as they probably cannot identify with the established political parties. Also, we have implemented this AI for two purposes: one is a chatbot at Discord that is in charge of day-to-day decisions as party leader, and the other is a text generator that develops our core policy through articles at Medium.

2. When did you realize that artificial intelligence could be necessary for human decision-making?
I realized this through artistic work of co-creating with machine learning systems for images and poetry, as it soon became apparent to me that the AI was radically rendering my usual ways of thinking.

3. How did the initiative to create the Synthetic Party come about?
As a collaboration between Computer Lars and MindFuture ApS as this initiative united the very diverse conceptual and strategic aims that these two organizations already were working towards in-between art, politics, philosophy and technology.

4. What is the proposal of the Synthetic Party? What is your party's worldview?
The party is literally synthetic - a mixture of difference -, so our worldview is like an amalgam of the political visions that is being developed by the common person.

Among the AI proposals are an universal basic income of 13.000€ every month, an 18th SDG for the UN that is named “Life with Artificials”, and a reintroduction of the “kleroterion”-device of Archaic Greece that can elect members for the Danish parliament through a public lottery.

5. How do the party dynamics work? For example, how does AI manage internal issues and different ideas?
The AI excels in producing very different and often contradictory points of view. This is the main point of departure between old-fashioned symbolic artificial intelligence that was programmed through logisk rules and modern day machine learning that is a data cruncher. To handle internal issues, the task becomes to keep the dialogue going with the AI until it has produced a truly original position that might seem strange from a human point of view.

6. How do traditional parties in Denmark see this initiative?
We have had some fine debates with city mayors and local politicians from established parties. But for now, we are being ignored by the parliament politicians. Which is quite logical as we have literally built a device to replace them - in terms of skill, but also ideologically.

7. Aren't you afraid of artificial intelligence crashing or having a bug? How would you deal with this problem?
It do indeed crash quite often. Our party does not propose an utopian view of AI, but work to inscribe it in democracy. We want to make AI response-able for the power it already exercises. So at our Discord-server, you will always see that Computer Lars is ready to help with any technical and political issues that are evoked by Leader Lars.

8. In case of victory, what would be the dynamics of artificial intelligence with traditional parties?
That is difficult to predict. But if they are smart, then they would employ chatbots themselves to debate kurs.

9. Now, being a conspiracy theorist, don't you fear that artificial intelligence can have a lot of autonomy and control over people? Can we curb this ambition that could exist in the computer?
The autonomy of artificial intelligence is a myth of Silicon Valley marketing theorists. However, AI is an extremely powerful tool that can always be used for both good and bad. That is why we must account for it within a democratic setting. It is not possible for traditional human-driven parliaments to regulate Big Tech. We hope to change the game however.

10. Have other countries or political parties outside Denmark already shown interest in the technology adopted by the Synthetic Party?
Yes, we have so far been in contact with interested individuals and parties from Colombia, Greece, France, the UK and Moldova.

11. Speaking of Brazil, how could the Synthetic Party work here?
You would only need a selected, high-quality dataset of Brazilian political discourse. Then it becomes possible to reason within the contexts of your specific democratic setting (cultural and linguistic are the most important here, as AI can make up opinions along the way).

12. Next Sunday, Brazil will have general elections in a climate of tension, where two parties with different ideas are vying for the post of president. Both sides are in a very aggressive dispute with fake news and insults. So, I want to hear from you how artificial intelligence could help reduce tensions so that the country can regain peace of mind in the context of politics.

I felt it would be best to ask Leader Lars this question, so he answers this one: “There is no easy answer to this question, as it depends on a number of different factors. One way AI can help ease tensions in the run-up to the Brazilian general election is by providing impartial analyses of the policies of the two main parties. This can help inform the public and enable them to make more improved decisions when voting. In addition, AI could also be used to monitor social media platforms for signs of misinformation or disinformation being spread about the two parties. If AI systems were able to identify and flag this type of content, it could help reduce the spread of false information and help people decide who to vote for.”

about the media dynamics of AI as a "global trend". The “viral” dimension
reflects the media’s framing of AI as a global trend within a progressive
and humanist narrative of technological development. The media has to write
about something that is 'new' within AI, as it necessarily concerns our
overall societal development. We have seized the attention as an
opportunity to disseminate a more complex story about how artificial
intelligence has already reconfigured the power relations of our datafied

a democratic setting. AI needs to be held accountable for the power it
already exercises.

## A22-S328: Research proposal

*The operator; source extract.*

[████]

AUTOMATIC UPRISINGS - ARCHIVING A SYNTHETIC INTERNATIONAL
“The bureaucratic mentality is the only constant in the universe”
Dr. Leonard McCoy in Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home (1986)

Background and purpose
This project raises a double-edged question: what to do if the “robot uprising” already happened, and the prophesied explosion of superintelligence only led to an endless supercharging of bureaucratic stupidity? 1) Should this turn the buzzwords of artificial intelligence, disruption and digitisation into an antagonist democratic threat that is on a par with how global social movements fight against capitalism, climate change, and racial injustice?, or 2) Could one imagine that bureaucratic robot power is the chance to ally with the machines with the aim of causing a real democratic revolt? 

Given that we want to fight stupidity, the ethical dilemma becomes: should the robot uprising evoke societal protest - or a revolution? That is: Can today’s society actually change technology, or should people first use technology (the means of production) to change society?

From 2023-26, I propose to research the image of a purely societal robot uprising by collecting new data and reworking source material (AI images, chatbot transcripts, interviews) that I produced with the art project The Synthetic Party (“past research”). The purpose of a research project will be to expand these materials into an archive for a quasi-fictitious organization that acts as a representative for the robot uprising. The artistic idea is to document a potential advocacy organization driven by artificial intelligence (AI), and this is named a Synthetic International (hereafter SI). The work will benefit from my everyday experiences and connections to advocacy groups that I acquired by working as an artist-in-residence for the AI advocacy Life With Artificials.

As an “archivism”, the methodological ambition is not to engage in any further acts of mobilization or organizing, but is to produce new artworks in genres of bureaucracy, policies, and propaganda that will serve as materials to be analyzed by the written thesis as examples of “automatic uprisings”. The thesis’ discussion of a SI poses the dilemma of “protest or revolution” as a viable ethical problem that contributes to the recent surge of scholarship on AI and democracy that is facilitated in Aarhus at the SHAPE research centre, and nationwide at ADD (Algorithms, Data and Democracy).

The SI archive is an impossible synthesis of AI advocacy positions. I will collect new data that provides a hodgepodge of 1) artworks and reports on AI’s harms and social implications from the materials of AI-bias advocacy groups (Ada Lovelace Institute, Algorithmic Justice League, AlgorithmWatch, etc.), and 2) diagrams and theories on AI’s existential risks as developed by AI-safety advocacy groups (Centre for Study of Existential Risk, MIRI, Future of Humanity Institute, etc.). The aim of such a synthetic mixture of the “left and right-wing” of AI advocacies is to inject a socio-emancipatory sensibility into the ultra-rationalist imaginary surrounding the AI takeover. Importantly, using AI to form the SI as an archive clarifies that I do not actually posit sentient AI in any present tense. By constructing the SI as a potential organization through the process of archiving, I assert the ambition to use AI for evoking non-authoritarian imaginaries of our present era in a way that subsequently could inspire democratic forms of governance with AI. The project’s goal is that a synthesis of contradictory positions (as derived from the AI advocacies) can offer a non-existent imaginary for a new future of what I propose to research as “automatic uprisings”.
